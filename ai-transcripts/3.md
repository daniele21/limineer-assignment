The rubric gives us a **screening score from 0–100**. We should preserve its weights exactly, while showing evidence completeness and recommendation risks alongside the score.

I applied it to all 23 normalized candidates and saved the [detailed review](./analysis/SCORING_RUBRIC_REVIEW.md) and [per-site calculations](./output/rubric_review.json).

Each criterion contributes:

\[
\text{contribution}=\text{weight}\times\frac{\text{tier}}{5}
\]

The practical point values are:

| Criterion | Maximum | Input → points |
|---|---:|---|
| Grid headroom | 30 | ≥4 MW → **30**; 2–<4 → **18**; 1–<2 → **6**; <1 → **0** |
| Substation distance | 15 | ≤1 km → **15**; >1–3 → **9**; >3–6 → **3**; >6 → **0** |
| Owner status | 20 | LOI signed → **20**; in talks → **12**; not contacted → **4**; refused → **0** |
| Community/council sentiment | 10 | Supportive → **10**; neutral → **6**; opposed → **0** |
| Flood zone | 15 | None → **15**; low → **12**; medium → **6**; high → **0** |
| Usable-area proxy | 10 | ≥1,200 m² → **10**; 800–<1,200 → **4**; <800 → **0** |

Exactly **2 MW earns 18 points**, and exactly **1,200 m² earns 10**. Distance thresholds are inclusive. These rules remove the overlapping wording in the original rubric.

The weights allocate **45 points to grid**, **30 to owner/local acceptance**, and **25 to flood/area**. However, their effects are uneven: crossing 4 MW adds 12 points, gaining an LOI from an owner already in talks adds eight, and moving from neutral sentiment to supportive adds four.

That creates several important consequences for our dataset.

**First, strengths can compensate for serious weaknesses.** S-006 and S-007 each score **76**, despite having only 1.7 and 1.6 MW of official headroom. The rubric permits that; our existing assumption also permits smaller batteries. Their scores do not establish suitability for a 2 MW installation.

Similarly, opposition costs at most ten points and high flood risk costs fifteen. Protection is different: it is an eligibility exclusion applied before scoring.

**Second, thresholds create abrupt jumps.** A change from 3.9 to 4.0 MW adds 12 points, even though the underlying capacity changes only slightly. We should implement the agreed thresholds directly and flag values near consequential boundaries for verification.

**Third, more capacity or land eventually stops adding value.** A 5.5 MW site receives the same headroom points as a 4.1 MW site. Eighteen of our 23 candidates already receive the maximum area contribution. Extra area should not become an undocumented tie-breaker or bonus.

For the nine sites with unknown or conditional sentiment, I recommend retaining a **null tier and null contribution**, then calculating:

- **Lower bound:** contributions from known criteria.
- **Upper bound:** lower bound plus the missing criterion’s maximum ten points.

Unknown sentiment contributes no earned points to the lower bound, but it is never labelled opposition. We must also avoid rescaling the known 90% of weights to 100%, which would reward missing evidence.

These ranges describe possible rubric completion. They are **not confidence intervals** and do not capture stale grid data or uncertainty in the parcel-area proxy.
