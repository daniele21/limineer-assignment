# Maren's shortlist

Visual thesis: the brief's cream, brown, amber and teal palette creates a warm decision sheet. Teal identifies interactive controls. Forest green marks high fit and recorded positives, amber flags assumptions and cautions, and warm red identifies low fit, recorded opposition, high flood risk and firm exclusions. Amber gradients emphasise rank without overwhelming the list.

Content plan: one prominent “Make this shortlist more accurate” action, then five compact rows containing rank, name, region and an actionable decision label. Explanations, comparisons, next checks and the rubric stay behind explicit disclosure controls.

Interaction thesis: short list-entry fades orient the initial scan; inline detail reveals preserve context; a question sheet exposes one decision at a time and reports changes to the shortlist immediately. Respect reduced-motion preferences.

## Information hierarchy

1. **Scan:** rank, site name, region and an actionable label. Recorded strong sites say “Start site checks”; unresolved support says “Confirm local support”; unfinished owner talks say “Secure owner intent”; assumed capacity says “Verify grid capacity”. These are screening actions, not construction approvals. A short High / Medium / Low fit band summarises the current score; the exact number remains in the breakdown. On mobile the band shares the action row; on desktop it occupies the right of the header. Bands are display summaries, not additional sponsor criteria: High ≥80, Medium 60–79, Low <60. They do not change ranking or eligibility, or imply construction readiness. The breakdown explains these boundaries. Colour fades vertically through the five cards from a strong amber first place to an almost neutral fifth place. Each card has a uniform fill. Tint follows current rank, moderated by absolute score, and recalculates with each answer. Ties receive equal emphasis; tint ranges from 0.01 to 0.27.
2. **Understand:** “Why this recommendation” opens a concise priority explanation and three qualitative evidence groups: grid connection, agreements, and land/flood. Short phrases such as “Strong capacity · nearby substation” and “Signed owner intent · support unknown” explain the result without numerical sub-scores. Assumed and unknown inputs remain explicit. The next check follows. Mobile pairs each evidence category with its explanation; desktop places the three groups side by side to use the available width.
3. **Inspect:** “See score breakdown” opens a site-specific dialog with exact totals, six weighted criteria and the numerical rank explanation. Dated field notes remain behind “Read the source evidence”. The dialog fills mobile screens and is centred on desktop; closing restores focus and shortlist position.
4. **Explore:** “Make this shortlist more accurate” opens four multiple-choice questions. Each answer immediately selects its offline scoring profile. Unanswered questions retain conservative defaults. Reset restores the evidence-based shortlist.

“Compare scenarios” is off on each page load. When enabled, a short guide states what is being compared: Now uses selected answers or conservative defaults; Potential assumes favourable outcomes for unverified checks; Evidence only uses official figures and recorded positions. Two buttons select the target scenario without changing the current shortlist.

Each site compares the same dimension: “Leading choice”, “Shortlisted option”, “Backup option”, or a critical-check/exclusion status. A two-column Now → target layout, a “Priority changes” heading and a short “Why” sentence explain the consequence. Fit bands appear on both sides only when the band changes; identical bands get a single “Fit stays high” statement. The explanation distinguishes changes in the site's assumed evidence from relative movement caused by other sites improving. Action labels remain independent of rank. Unchanged results use “Now = Potential” (or Evidence only), with “No change in recommendation” and one summary. Exact scores remain in the breakdown dialog. The comparison stays active during question updates until switched off or reloaded.

“Why this shortlist is defensible” leads with consistent criteria, recorded evidence and explicit assumptions. Formulae and weights require a further “Inspect the scoring method” disclosure. Numeric scores remain available for audit: they are out of 100, not confidence percentages. Unknown support produces a lower bound; held sites have no ranked score, rather than zero.

The same compact single-column hierarchy is used on mobile and desktop. The mobile question sheet fills the screen; desktop uses a modal. Controls have at least 44 px touch targets. Meeting guidance, alternatives and scoring methodology are collapsed by default.

Short score explanations emphasise recorded maximum-tier inputs with bold forest-green text and a soft highlight. Tier-four positives, such as low flood risk, use semibold forest green without the maximum-tier highlight. Confirmed refusal, opposition and high flood risk use red. Assumptions, unknowns and other low-tier limitations use amber, even when an assumption earns maximum points. Other recorded inputs remain muted. The wording remains explicit so interpretation does not depend on colour alone.

Conditional opportunities have their own disclosed list. Excluded owners and protected sites never re-enter the recommendations. Equal scores share a competition rank; site ID only determines display order. No answer changes underlying evidence.

## Sites outside the five

The outside browser offers three groups: contenders, sites needing checks, and firm exclusions. Only one group and three records appear initially, with progressive “Show 3 more” navigation. Contenders retain current-score order. The pending-check group combines held and conditional opportunities and sorts by the fixed risky scenario score; these remain outside the shortlist.

Opening a contender immediately explains why stronger options made the five and its main limitations, without numeric point gaps. Pending-check sites show the specific blocker in the collapsed row, then reveal the eligibility reason and evidence-backed strengths when opened. Firm exclusions state the recorded refusal or protected status. The full rubric remains in a dialog; one outside-site disclosure opens at a time.
